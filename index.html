FAQ

Questions about starting therapy.

How do I find the right therapist in BC?

Start with what you'd like help with and whether you'd prefer to meet in person or online. Read a few profiles, then book a short consultation if one is offered. Training and approach matter, but research consistently shows that the relationship itself matters most, so pay attention to whether you feel safe and understood. My guide on choosing a therapist walks through what to ask.

What's the difference between a counsellor, psychotherapist and psychologist in BC?

These titles mean different things in BC, both in training and in how they're regulated, and some will change when counselling becomes a regulated profession. Knowing the difference can help you check credentials and understand what your benefits cover.

How much does therapy cost, and are there low-cost options?

MSP doesn't cover private counselling, but there are free and low-cost options across BC, including community counselling programs, training clinics with supervised student counsellors, and sliding scale fees. Many extended health plans also cover counselling.

What happens in a first therapy session?

Most first sessions are a chance to talk about what brings you in, what you're hoping for and a bit of your history. You don't need to know what to say or have everything figured out. It's also a chance to ask your own questions and notice how it feels to talk with this person.

Which type of therapy is right for me?

Many approaches can help, from talk therapy to EMDR, IFS and body-based work. The guides on this site explain how each one works so you can get a feel for what might suit you. If you're unsure, a good therapist can help you figure it out together.
