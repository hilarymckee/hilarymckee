Start where you are

You don’t need a crisis to begin.

Whether you’re struggling in a relationship or struggling to feel like yourself, you deserve a space where all of you is welcome. People often start therapy for reasons like these.

  • Feeling anxious, low or burnt out, and not sure why
  • Having the same argument with your partner over and over
  • Caught in people-pleasing, perfectionism or a harsh inner critic
  • Untangling what you learned growing up
  • Simply wanting to understand yourself better

Leave these behind

Therapy myths I’d like to retire.

Tap a card to see what it’s really like.

The only real requirement: showing up.

Read all five myths and answers
  • “You lie on a couch and talk about your mother.” You can sit however you like. Your mother is optional.
  • “If I cry, I’ll look like a mess.” You won’t be the first person to ugly cry in that chair today. Possibly not even the first within the hour.
  • “Your therapist will tell you what to do.” Good therapy is more like having someone help you hear yourself think.
  • “I have to know what to talk about.” “I don’t know where to start” is a great place to start.
  • “Once I start, I’m stuck with that therapist.” You’re allowed to shop around. Fit matters more than anything.

Questions about starting therapy

How do I find the right therapist in BC?
Start with what you’d like help with and whether you’d prefer to meet in person or online. Read a few profiles, then book a short consultation if one is offered. Pay attention to whether you feel safe and understood. My guide on choosing a therapist walks through what to ask.
What’s the difference between a counsellor, psychotherapist and psychologist in BC?
These titles mean different things in BC, both in training and in how they’re regulated.
How much does therapy cost, and are there low-cost options?
MSP doesn’t cover private counselling, but there are free and low-cost options across BC, including community programs, training clinics and sliding scale fees.
What happens in a first therapy session?
We go over consent, confidentiality and how supervision works, then talk about what brings you in, at whatever pace feels okay. You don’t need to know what to say. You can also ask me anything about my training or how I work.
Which type of therapy is right for me?
Many approaches can help. The guides on this site explain how each one works, and a good therapist can help you figure out what fits.
