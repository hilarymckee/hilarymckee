Free and low-cost counselling in BC: a practical guide.

Hilary McKee Starting Therapy 7 min read Updated October 19, 2026

The short answer

MSP doesn't cover private counselling in BC, but you still have options. Community organizations offer free or low-cost counselling across the province, and training clinics offer sessions with supervised graduate students on a sliding scale. There are free programs for youth, post-secondary students, Indigenous people, newcomers and 2SLGBTQ+ people. You can also ask any counsellor whether they offer a sliding scale. If you're not sure where to start, call 310-6789 (no area code) for information on services near you.

Cost is one of the biggest reasons people put off therapy. When a single session can cost as much as a week of groceries, it's easy to decide you'll deal with it later.

The good news is that there are more free and low-cost options in BC than most people realize. They can be hard to find, so I've gathered the main ones here.

What MSP does and doesn't cover

If you're eligible for MSP, mental health services in the public system are usually covered. That includes your local mental health centre or team and outpatient psychiatry at a hospital. Your family doctor can tell you whether one of these might be right for you.

What MSP doesn't cover is private counselling or psychotherapy, which is where most people find a counsellor. For that, you'll need to pay yourself, use benefits or look at the options below.

Free counselling in BC

Community counselling programs (for adults across BC)

The Community Counselling Fund, administered by the Community Action Initiative, supports organizations across BC to offer free and low-cost counselling, often in familiar places like neighbourhood houses and friendship centres. In 2025, the province announced $6.7 million in continued funding for 44 organizations.

You can find a list of the organizations at caibc.ca/ccfprofiles. Eligibility and services vary, so contact the one nearest you to ask.

One example in Metro Vancouver is S.U.C.C.E.S.S., which has offered free funded counselling, with a sliding scale for other services, in English, Cantonese, Mandarin, Korean and Farsi.

BounceBack (adults with low mood, stress or anxiety)

BounceBack is a free skill-building program run by the Canadian Mental Health Association. It's designed for people dealing with low mood, mild to moderate depression, anxiety, stress or worry. You can use the self-guided online program or videos without a referral. The phone coaching option, which includes 4 to 6 sessions with a coach, needs a referral from a doctor or nurse practitioner.

BounceBack isn't therapy with a counsellor, but it can be a helpful place to start, especially while you're on a waitlist.

Foundry (ages 12 to 24)

Foundry offers free mental health support for young people aged 12 to 24 and their families, in centres across BC and online through Foundry Virtual BC. You can book same-day or scheduled counselling by chat, video or phone through the Foundry BC app or website, or by calling 1-833-308-6379. No referral is needed.

Here2Talk (post-secondary students)

Here2Talk offers free, 24/7 counselling by phone and chat to students registered at a BC college, university, institute or trades program. Call 1-877-857-3397 or visit here2talk.ca. Your school's counselling centre is another free option worth checking.

Indigenous people

The First Nations Health Authority offers a mental health and counselling benefit for First Nations people in BC, along with other mental health programs. Métis people can access the Métis Counselling Connection Program through Métis Nation BC. The KUU-US Crisis Line (1-800-588-8717) offers culturally aware crisis support, and Hope for Wellness offers support for Indigenous people across Canada.

Newcomers

In Vancouver, MOSAIC supports newcomers, and the Multicultural Mental Health Resource Centre shares information in several languages, including Chinese, Arabic, Farsi and Spanish.

2SLGBTQ+ people

Qmunity can help queer, trans and Two-Spirit people find free or low-cost, queer-competent services. Mind Map BC also lists many services, with a filter to show only free ones.

People affected by violent crime

If you've been hurt by a violent crime in BC, or you're an immediate family member or certain witness, the Crime Victim Assistance Program (CVAP) may cover counselling. You don't have to wait for charges or a conviction to apply. For crimes on or after January 1, 2024, you generally have two years to apply. Call 1-866-660-3888 to learn more.

Low-cost counselling in BC

Training clinics and intern counsellors

This is one of the best-kept secrets in BC. Counsellors in training, usually graduate students completing a master's or doctoral degree, see clients at a reduced cost while working under the supervision of experienced, registered professionals.

A few examples in Vancouver:

  • UBC Psychology Clinic: Doctoral students in clinical psychology, supervised by registered psychologists, offer therapy on a sliding scale. Individual sessions have been listed at $10 to $50, and group therapy at $5 to $25.
  • The Adler Centre: Master's-level counsellors completing their internships offer individual, couples and family counselling. Sessions have been listed at $65, with a sliding scale available.

Many private clinics also have intern counsellors who offer lower rates. When you're searching, look for words like "intern," "student counsellor" or "reduced cost," or simply ask the clinic.

I'm a student counsellor myself at East Vancouver Counselling on Commercial Drive, which offers low-cost counselling in person and online across BC.

Is it worth seeing a student? In my view, often yes. Interns are closely supervised, usually have more time to prepare for each session and are learning the most current approaches. The most important thing in therapy is feeling safe with the person you're working with, and that has nothing to do with years of experience.

Sliding scale counselling

Many counsellors in private practice keep a few spots at a lower rate, based on income. It's not always advertised, so it's worth asking. Here's one way to say it:

"I'm interested in working with you, but cost is a concern for me. Do you offer a sliding scale or any reduced-fee spots?"

You don't need to explain your finances in detail or apologize for asking. Therapists hear this question often, and if they can't help, many will know someone who can.

Low-cost couples counselling in BC

Couples counselling is often harder to find at a lower cost, and private sessions usually cost more than individual ones. A few places to look:

  • Training clinics: The Adler Centre's intern counsellors see couples as well as individuals, and sessions have been listed at $65 with a sliding scale available.
  • Intern counsellors at private clinics: Many clinics have interns who work with couples at reduced rates. Ask whether an intern is available, and whether they have training in couples approaches like Gottman or Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT).
  • Community organizations: Some community counselling programs offer couple and family counselling. S.U.C.C.E.S.S., for example, has offered couple therapy as part of its funded counselling.
  • Your benefits: Check whether your extended health plan covers couples sessions, and whether you and your partner can each use your own plan.

If cost means only one of you can start right now, individual counselling can still help. Changing how you show up in a relationship often changes the relationship itself.

Ways to make private therapy more affordable

  • Check your extended health benefits. Many workplace plans cover counselling, sometimes through a partner's or parent's plan. Ask which designations your plan covers, such as RCC (Registered Clinical Counsellor), registered psychologist or RSW (registered social worker), and how much per year.
  • Use your Employee and Family Assistance Program (EFAP). Many employers offer a few free counselling sessions through an EFAP. Check with HR or your benefits booklet.
  • Try group therapy. Groups are usually much cheaper than individual sessions and can be powerful in their own right.
  • Space out your sessions. Once you're settled with a counsellor, meeting every two or three weeks can make therapy easier to sustain.
  • Keep your receipts. Some larger health costs can be claimed on your income tax return. Check what qualifies before you file.

If you're not sure where to start

  • Call 310-6789 (no area code) for emotional support and information on mental health services near you.
  • Call or text 2-1-1 to find local services.
  • Call 8-1-1 (HealthLink BC) for free health information and advice.
  • Talk to your family doctor or nurse practitioner about options in the public system.

If you can choose between a few options, my guide on how to choose the right therapist for you can help.

If you're in crisis

If you're thinking about suicide or you're in crisis, call or text 9-8-8 any time, or call the BC Crisis Line at 1-800-784-2433. In an emergency, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Does MSP cover counselling in BC?

MSP covers mental health services in the public system, like local mental health teams and outpatient hospital psychiatry. It does not cover private counselling or psychotherapy with a counsellor in private practice.

Where can I get free counselling in BC?

Free options include community counselling programs funded through the Community Counselling Fund, Foundry for ages 12 to 24, Here2Talk for post-secondary students, BounceBack for low mood and anxiety, and programs for Indigenous people, newcomers and 2SLGBTQ+ people. Call 310-6789 for help finding services near you.

What is a sliding scale in counselling?

A sliding scale means the fee changes based on your income, so people with lower incomes pay less. Many counsellors and clinics offer this, even if it isn't advertised. It's always okay to ask.

Are student or intern counsellors any good?

Intern counsellors are graduate students who are supervised by experienced, registered professionals. Many people have a great experience with them, and the reduced cost can make regular sessions possible. What matters most is feeling safe and understood by the person you work with.

Is there low-cost couples counselling in Vancouver?

Yes. Training clinics like the Adler Centre have listed couples sessions with supervised intern counsellors at about $65, with a sliding scale available. Many private clinics also have interns who see couples at a reduced rate, so it's worth asking.

How much does counselling cost in Vancouver?

Prices vary a lot. Some community programs are free, training clinics have listed sessions from about $10 to $65, and private counselling often costs more. Ask any counsellor about their fees and whether they offer reduced-cost spots.

The bottom line

Cost is a real barrier, and it shouldn't be the reason you go without support. Start with one call or one email. You deserve care, and there's more of it available than you might think.
