Starting therapy

What happens in a first therapy session?
  1. You arrive, or log on. In person on Commercial Drive or online from anywhere in BC. Sit however you like.
  2. We cover the basics. Consent, confidentiality and how supervision works, in plain language.
  3. You share what brings you in. As much or as little as feels okay.
  4. You ask me anything. About my training, my approach or what working together could look like.
Do I need to know what to talk about?
No. “I don’t know where to start” is a great place to start. Part of my job is helping you find the thread.
Which type of therapy is right for me?
Many approaches can help. My therapy guides explain how each one works, and we can figure out together what fits.
Do I need a crisis to start therapy?
Not at all. Wanting to understand yourself better is reason enough.

Working with me

What does it mean that you’re a counselling intern?
I’m completing my Master of Arts in Counselling Psychology at Yorkville University. From January 2027 I’ll see clients at East Vancouver Counselling, supervised by Kennedy Smith, CCC. My guide to seeing a student counsellor in BC explains how this works.
Is therapy with you confidential?
Yes, with the same limits that apply to any counsellor, such as when someone’s safety is at serious risk. I discuss my work with my supervisor, who is also bound by confidentiality, and I’ll explain all of this in our first session.
Do you work with couples?
Yes. My couples work is Gottman-informed, and I also see individuals who want to understand their relationship patterns.
Do you offer online sessions?
Yes, anywhere in BC, as well as in person on Commercial Drive.

Cost and finding a therapist

How much does a session cost?
Intern sessions at East Vancouver Counselling are $60 plus GST, with a sliding scale available. Couples sessions are also offered at a reduced intern rate. Ask about current fees in your free consultation.
Are there free or low-cost options in BC?
Yes. MSP doesn’t cover private counselling, but there are free and low-cost options across BC, including community programs, training clinics and sliding scale fees.
How do I find the right therapist in BC?
Start with what you’d like help with and whether you’d prefer in person or online. Book a short consultation and notice whether you feel safe and understood. My guide on choosing a therapist walks through what to ask.
What’s the difference between a counsellor, psychotherapist and psychologist?
These titles mean different things in BC, both in training and in how they’re regulated.
